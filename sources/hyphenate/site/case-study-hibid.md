01 · Challenge

## Dual business model outgrew manual order-to-cash processes

When Chris Stiegal joined HiBid in January 2025, he stepped into a high-volume finance operation whose manual processes couldn’t keep pace with growth. Alongside its marketplace, the company offers vertical software that auctioneers use to manage customer relationships, inventory, and operations. With revenue spanning four major streams and more than 50 sub-streams, modernizing revenue and cash workflows became increasingly important.

Managing the complexity of this business model took days each month. Each stream is recognized on a different schedule, requiring the team to export hundreds of thousands of transaction line items and manually calculate balances in Excel. This work pulled time away from the variance analysis and budget-versus-actuals reporting needed to understand HiBid’s performance and plan liquidity.

Because the workbook reached 300 MB, processing also required dedicated computing resources, hard-coded formulas, and careful file management to prevent recalculation errors.

That challenge extended to cash reconciliation. HiBid billed customers in advance, at the point of sale, and in arrears, while payments arrived by check, wire, ACH, and multiple payment processors. With billing and payment data spread across systems and payment methods, the team had to manually determine what had been collected, what remained in accounts receivable (AR), and what was still in transit.

Without a consolidated view of the data, Chris and his team found it harder to prepare for audits, spot deeper financial performance trends, and build granular forecasts. In turn, this delayed decision-making around capital allocation and future growth initiatives, including product investment and go-to-market priorities.

Chris had attempted to integrate the company’s data warehouse with NetSuite to streamline order-to-cash operations. But even after nearly three months and significant engineering support, the system could not reliably handle HiBid’s transaction volume, varied revenue streams, and complex recognition rules.

As an early adopter of AI agents, Chris had seen their potential to accelerate financial analysis and automate routine work. However, after experimenting with generic AI tools like Codex and Claude Code, he found they couldn’t consistently produce the controlled, accurate, and auditable data required for HiBid’s order-to-cash workflows.

Faced with these constraints, Chris began exploring AI-native solutions that could layer on top of HiBid’s systems. He evaluated several platforms, but none combined the ability to learn from HiBid’s policies and prior work, the accuracy required for its financial operations, and the support for its disparate data sources and complex accounting practices.

Chris found the right partner in Hyphenate. Its multi-source data integration, AI-native capabilities, and deep accounting knowledge provided the financial foundation for HiBid’s continued growth.

02 · Solution

## AI agents automate revenue recognition and cash reconciliation

HiBid integrated Hyphenate into its existing environment without disrupting the systems in place. From there, the Hyphenate team connected HiBid’s data sources, including its data warehouse, billing engine, and multiple payment processors. “I was amazed by how quickly they consolidated our data with little engineering effort,” says Chris.

Working closely with the HiBid team, Hyphenate then configured the platform around HiBid’s order-to-cash workflows, revenue-recognition policies, and business-specific exceptions. They also introduced a shared Slack channel and a Friday touch-base meeting to ensure smooth adoption. The result was a unified finance context layer that enables Hyphenate agents to handle HiBid’s order-to-cash processes.

““The Hyphenate team became true partners from the start,” Chris shares. “They took joint ownership of our close process and exchanged ideas with us on how to introduce automation in the right places.””

Chris Stiegal

CFO at HiBid

As HiBid brought the new workflow into day-to-day use, Chris compared revenue-recognition data in Hyphenate. The review revealed a small variance between Hyphenate calculations and HiBid’s prior numbers. This enabled the team to improve the accuracy of its revenue recognition, giving Chris greater confidence in Hyphenate output and the accuracy of HiBid’s financials.

Today, Hyphenate agents continuously pull transaction reports from the data warehouse and billing engine, match them to the right revenue stream, and apply the right recognition policies. With this repeatable workflow in place, Chris and his finance team have a granular, reliable view of recognized and deferred revenue by customer, product, and revenue stream.

When Hyphenate encounters an unfamiliar transaction, it flags the exception for HiBid’s finance team and provides the relevant context for review. The team can then explain the appropriate treatment in plain language without extensive manual investigation. That guidance becomes part of HiBid’s operating context, enabling the agents to handle similar scenarios independently.

As Hyphenate takes on more of HiBid’s routine revenue-recognition work, the finance team can spend more time on higher-value work, such as variance analysis and budget-versus-actuals reporting. As a result, the team can give leadership earlier visibility into the revenue streams driving performance, where results are diverging from plan, and where to focus additional investment.
