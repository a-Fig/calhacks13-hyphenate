# How human professions make delegated work trustworthy and auditable

Researched 2026-10-24 by a Sonnet research agent.

**Epistemic status.** Quoted or paraphrased text marked (read) was fetched on 2026-10-24: ICD 203 (full text, archive.dni.gov PDF), 14 CFR 43.9 (Cornell LII), and secondary summaries of PCAOB AS 1215 (the pcaobus.org pages returned 403, so AS 1215 paragraph numbers come from a secondary summary and search snippets). ICD 206 is a scanned PDF I could not text-extract. Everything marked (memory) is my recollection and should be checked before it goes on a slide. Studies I only saw in search snippets are marked (snippet).

## 1. Bottom line

Every mature profession converged on the same small set of ideas, and our architecture already has about half of them.

1. **Trust is built by making the work re-performable by a stranger, not by making the author credible.** PCAOB AS 1215's test is that documentation lets "an experienced auditor, having no previous connection with the engagement" understand the procedures, evidence and conclusions, and see who did and reviewed the work and when (read, secondary). That one sentence is the best acceptance test we can adopt.
2. **Facts, assumptions, judgments and computations are kept visibly separate.** ICD 203 (read), SOAP notes, lab notebooks and journalism all do this. Our `Assumption.source` (`given/default/agent_estimated`) is the right start, but the S5 narration and the agent's reasoning need the same labelling.
3. **Delegation is a table of limits with escalation, not a switch.** Corporate delegation-of-authority matrices, FAA designee authority limits and the aviation "recommend vs approve" split all say: what you may decide, up to what size, under what conditions, and who you hand to otherwise. Configurable agent autonomy should be a matrix, versioned, with cumulative caps.
4. **The record is written by someone other than the person being checked, and sign-offs bind a specific version.** Flight recorders, FAA conformity statements (read, search result), return-to-service signatures (read), audit sign-offs.
5. **The recurring failure is not missing logs, it is logs nobody reads and reviewers who stop checking.** Alert override rates of 49-96% (snippet), surgical checklists that helped in one study and not in another, automation complacency, and the 737 MAX delegation findings. Design the trail so the exceptions come first and so that reviewers are periodically tested.

What we are missing today, judged against `norn/ARCHITECTURE.md`: no approval-limit matrix (only a vague "agents sometimes approve small changes"), no record of what was searched and not found, no independent second read for auto-approvals, no human sign-off record bound to a forecast hash, no cumulative-limit logic to stop splitting, and nothing that tracks spawned researchers as accountable seats (issue #25).

## 2. Patterns by field

### 2.1 Financial audit (PCAOB AS 1215 and relatives)
- **Mechanism.** Working papers must let an experienced auditor with no prior connection to the engagement understand the nature, timing, extent and results of procedures, the evidence obtained and the conclusions (AS 1215 .06/.06A; read, secondary). "Experienced" means a reasonable understanding of audit activities and the client's industry. The test dates from government auditing, where GAO reviewers audit the audit without taking part (PCAOB Appendix A, via search snippet). The file shows who performed the work and the date, and who reviewed it and the date. Significant findings, actions taken and the basis for conclusions go in a completion document, by cross-reference if needed (.12-.13). Documentation is assembled by a completion date (45 days historically, 14 days under the 2024 amendments; effective dates conflicting in sources), retained seven years (.14), and later additions must say who, when and why, with nothing deleted (.16). Supervision and an engagement quality review (AS 1201, AS 1220; memory) add a second partner who was not on the engagement.
- **Craft, not codified in the standard (memory).** Tickmarks: a legend of symbols on each working paper (agreed to source, footed, cross-footed, traced to trial balance); every number on the page carries one. Cross-referencing: each paper has an index number, and numbers carry the reference of the paper they came from or feed. Review notes: the reviewer raises a point, the preparer answers it with a pointer to the fix, and all points are cleared before partner sign-off.
- **Why it works.** It prevents "trust me" conclusions and prevents work that only the author can follow. The stranger standard forces the author to externalise context. The completion date and additions rule stop quiet back-filling after the fact.
- **Translation.**
  - Every figure on the impact card carries a tickmark glyph (from a legend) and a reference ID. Hover gives the claim; click opens the exact source row (ERP/PLM record id, version, retrieved-at) or the exact calculation node (code version, input hash, seed).
  - Legend of tickmarks: `traced to source row`, `recomputed by code`, `agent-estimated, unverified`, `human-confirmed`, `conflicts with another source`.
  - Header on every run, like a working paper: prepared by (agent seat + model + prompt version) and date, reviewed by (human or checker agent) and date.
  - Review notes as first-class objects: a human comment on an assumption must be answered (cleared) before an approval can attach.
  - Acceptance test: put someone who did not watch the run in front of the UI and ask five questions (what did it read, what did it assume and where from, how was this number calculated, why this decision within its authority, who else touched it). If any takes more than a couple of minutes, the trail has failed.

### 2.2 Intelligence analysis (ICD 203, ICD 206)
- **Mechanism (ICD 203, read).** Standards include: describe the quality and credibility of sources, data and methods, including "accuracy and completeness, possible denial and deception, age and continued currency", source access and "possible bias"; use ICD 206 source descriptors and encouraged "source summary statements" that say which sources matter most to key judgments. Express uncertainty as two separate things, likelihood of the event and the analyst's confidence in the basis, and never combine a confidence level and a likelihood in one sentence. Likelihood uses fixed words with fixed ranges: almost no chance 01-05%, very unlikely 05-20%, unlikely 20-45%, roughly even 45-55%, likely 55-80%, very likely 80-95%, almost certain 95-99%. "Clearly distinguish" underlying information from assumptions and judgments; assumptions are "suppositions used to frame or support an argument", judgments are conclusions drawn from information, analysis and assumptions. State assumptions explicitly when they are the "linchpin" or bridge an information gap, say what follows if they are wrong, and name indicators that would change the judgment. Analyse alternative hypotheses. Explain change or consistency from prior judgments. Show significant contrary information. ICD 206 (memory) requires source reference citations on disseminated products so reviewers can find the underlying reporting.
- **Why it works.** The standards grew out of post-2001/2003 analytic failures (memory). They stop a confident-sounding conclusion from hiding a fragile assumption, and give consumers a shared vocabulary so "likely" means the same thing to everyone.
- **Translation.**
  - Three visible layers in the UI: Evidence (what the data says), Assumptions (with origin and impact-if-wrong), Judgment (the decision), each styled differently. S5 narration must tag each sentence with one layer.
  - Per assumption: origin (`given` row id / `default` rule / `agent_estimated` plus the comparables it used), rationale, "if wrong, the impact moves by X" taken from the sensitivity run, and an "indicator" that would change the agent's mind.
  - Separate display of likelihood (the p10/p50/p90 or probability of missing the effectivity date) from confidence (quality and quantity of evidence behind the inputs). A cost range built from three `agent_estimated` assumptions is wide and low confidence, and the card should say so.
  - Source summary statement per decision: "Rests mainly on supplier quote Q-123 (age 41 days) and an agent-estimated requalification duration."
  - An "alternatives considered" panel (e.g. second source, defer to next lot, reject) with why each was not chosen.
  - "What changed since last time" line when an ECR is re-assessed (Standard 7).

### 2.3 Aviation
- **Maintenance records (14 CFR 43.9(a), read).** Each entry has a description of the work or a reference to accepted data, completion date, the name of the person who did it (if different from the approver), and, if satisfactory, the approver's signature, certificate number and certificate kind. The signature "approves return to service only for the work listed". Aircraft record retention is in 14 CFR 91.417 (memory).
- **Conformity and delegation.** 14 CFR 21.33 requires inspection and tests; FAA Form 8130-9 statement of conformity must be signed by the party responsible for fabrication or assembly, not the designee who determines conformity (search result from FAA conformity plan). The person who made it and the person who checks it are different. DERs act for the FAA under Order 8110.37 (current G revision), which sets "limitations of authority"; selection, training and oversight sit in Order 8000.95 (both read via search). The FAA keeps final say and the authority of each designee is bounded by what they are authorised for (memory).
- **Checklists and recorders (memory).** Challenge-and-response checklists (one person reads the item, the other verifies and responds) originate in the 1935 Model 299 crash. Flight data and cockpit voice recorders are crash-protected, independent of the crew, and let investigators reconstruct what happened without anyone's account. CRM grew out of the 1978 United 173 fuel-exhaustion accident (NTSB AAR-79-7), where crew did not challenge the captain; it teaches explicit challenge, and an agreed way to escalate.
- **Why it works.** Authority is tied to a named person and a specific scope; the maker and the checker differ; the record is independent of those whose behaviour it records; speaking up is a procedure, not a personality trait.
- **Translation.**
  - Every agent approval is a "return to service" entry: scope (exact ECR + version hash it covers), what was done, who is accountable (human owner of that seat), authority row used. A later edit to the ECR or an assumption voids it, which `ARCHITECTURE.md` §8 already half-implements for PR approvals.
  - Platform-written flight recorder: the harness, not the agent, appends every tool call, source read, prompt, model response and result to an append-only log. The agent cannot edit or delete its own history.
  - Checker agent reads each critical item aloud in structured form ("assumption A7 = 14 weeks requal, source agent_estimated") and the maker confirms or disputes. Cheap form of challenge-and-response.
  - Delegation limits per seat, versioned, as in 2.8.

### 2.4 Medicine
- **Charting.** SOAP (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan) follows Weed's problem-oriented record (1968; memory). It separates what was observed from interpretation and plan. "Pertinent negatives" are recorded (what was checked and found absent). Corrections are addenda, not overwrites.
- **Second reads.** Breast-screening double reading with arbitration: two readers assess blind to each other, discordant cases go to consensus or a third reader. A 2018 English study of over 800,000 women found double reading with arbitration reduced recall and increased cancer detection versus single reading (snippet). Caveat from the same results: the second reader is often not fully blinded, which weakens independence.
- **Handoffs.** SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation; Navy, Kaiser; memory). I-PASS (Illness severity, Patient summary, Action list, Situation awareness and contingency, Synthesis by receiver) in Starmer et al., NEJM 2014;371:1803: medical errors fell about 23% and preventable adverse events about 30% across nine pediatric programs, with no added handoff time (snippet; pre/post, not randomised).
- **Review of failures.** Morbidity and mortality conferences review cases systematically, system-focused, protected from blame (memory).
- **Why it works.** Structure forces the sender to state severity, what was checked and what should happen next; the receiver's read-back catches misunderstanding; blind second reads catch individual misses; M&M converts incidents into process change.
- **Translation.**
  - Escalation messages to humans use SBAR: Situation (ECR-014 needs a decision, exceeds my $ limit), Background (what it touched), Assessment (my estimate, confidence, weakest assumption), Recommendation (approve with condition X). Plus "what I need from you" in one line.
  - Human-to-agent and agent-to-agent briefs use I-PASS shape: severity/priority, summary, action list, contingencies ("if supplier B quote arrives late, then..."), and a required read-back from the receiving agent before it starts.
  - Pertinent negatives panel: "searched the supplier file for alternates, none qualified; checked open ECRs touching P-4471, none."
  - Independent second read on auto-approvals: a checker with a different model or prompt that does not see the first agent's decision until it has formed its own, with disagreement routed to a human (the arbiter).
  - Monthly M&M view: auto-approved ECRs whose actuals diverged (Loop 2) and near misses where a human overrode the agent.

### 2.5 Law
- **Mechanism (memory unless noted).** Bates numbering stamps every produced page with a unique sequential ID so any statement can cite page-level evidence unambiguously. Chain of custody logs each hand-off of an exhibit (who, when, condition) to support authentication (Fed. R. Evid. 901). Citation norms: pinpoint cites, and signals (see, but see, cf., contra) that tell the reader how a source relates to the claim. Cite-checking and Shepardizing/KeyCite confirm the quoted authority exists and is still good law. A partner reviewing an associate's memo reads the Question, Brief Answer first, then spot-checks the key authorities and facts rather than rereading everything; the signing lawyer certifies reasonable inquiry (Fed. R. Civ. P. 11(b)) and answers personally.
- **A cautionary case (search results).** In Mata v. Avianca (S.D.N.Y. 2023), a brief contained at least six fabricated cases produced by ChatGPT; the court sanctioned the lawyers $5,000 and stressed the attorney's gatekeeping duty.
- **Why it works.** Stable IDs make claims checkable; custody logs make evidence admissible; signals separate support from contrary authority; Rule 11 attaches a human's name to the claim.
- **Translation.**
  - Stable IDs for every evidence item (`SRC-<system>-<record>@<version>`), and every claim cites them like a pinpoint cite.
  - Relation tags on each citation: `supports`, `contradicts`, `background`, `stale`. Show contradictions beside the claim, not buried.
  - Chain of custody for data: where each source row came from (ERP export, supplier PDF, agent-fetched web page), when retrieved, hash of the snapshot. Store snapshots, not links.
  - Mechanical cite-check before display: code verifies each quoted value or span exists in the cited snapshot; failed cites render red and block auto-approval. This directly prevents Mata-style fabrications.
  - "Currency check" (KeyCite analogue): when `main` or a quote changes, citations that rest on the old version are marked stale.
  - Memo format for the card: bottom line, brief answer with range, then support; the reviewer can start at the top and drill down only where unsure.

### 2.6 Science
- **Mechanism (memory unless noted).** Lab notebooks: bound, dated, ink, no erasures (strike through and initial), witnessed and signed, so they can establish who did what and when. Reproducibility packages: data, code, environment and seeds so a stranger can regenerate the result; Peng (Science 2011, snippet) frames a reproducibility spectrum from publication only to full replication, with shared code and data as the minimum. Peer review: reviewers see methods and data, authors answer point by point. Pre-registration and Registered Reports (snippet): the plan is peer-reviewed and fixed before data are collected, in-principle acceptance is granted, and analyses not in the plan are labelled exploratory.
- **Why it works.** Stops post hoc story-telling (changing the hypothesis after seeing the data), makes results checkable, and makes deviations visible rather than forbidden.
- **Translation.**
  - Our check runs already hash (base, overlay, assumptions, code version, seed) (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §3, §8). Expose a "re-run this number" button that reproduces it from stored inputs and shows that the hash matches. That is a reproducibility package at the click of a button.
  - Plan-first records: before computing, the agent writes its work plan (which sources, which method, which assumptions it expects to fill). The UI shows plan versus actual and flags deviations. Sensitivity and "what if" runs launched after seeing the answer are labelled `exploratory`.
  - Pre-commit decision rules: the auto-approve threshold applies to the number computed, and cannot be altered inside the same run.
  - Point-by-point response for human edits: when a reviewer changes an assumption, the system records what changed in the outputs and the agent's response.

### 2.7 Journalism
- **Mechanism (memory).** Newsroom standards (AP, Reuters, SPJ code) require attribution, preference for primary documents and on-the-record sources, and corroboration of consequential claims from more than one independent source. Dedicated fact-checkers (New Yorker, Der Spiegel Dokumentation) work through a draft fact by fact, marking each verified element and noting its source; checkers go back to the original source rather than the writer's notes. Corrections are published, not silently fixed.
- **Why it works.** Verification is separated from authorship, and the burden of proof falls on each claim. Independent corroboration catches single-source error.
- **Translation.**
  - "Two-source rule" for decision-driving inputs: lead time or price that moves the decision should have two independent sources (supplier quote and ERP purchase history); if they disagree, show both and flag `sources conflict` (Hyphenate's "sources that disagree").
  - Source hierarchy shown on each input: contract/PO > supplier quote > ERP history > agent estimate.
  - A fact-check pass per card: a checker marks each sentence of the narration verified or not, with unverified sentences visibly greyed until resolved.
  - Visible corrections log on each ECR: what was wrong, who found it, what was changed.

### 2.8 Delegation of authority (corporate finance)
- **Mechanism (partly read via secondary sources; COSO text itself not read).** A delegation-of-authority matrix lists decision types against roles with monetary or risk thresholds; above the limit the decision escalates to the next level. Better matrices state aggregate limits per period, who may sub-delegate and for how long, and how it is recorded. COSO's Internal Control framework lists authorisations and segregation of duties among control activities, and says no single person should initiate, authorise, record and review a transaction. Four-eyes review and no-self-approval rules follow. The FAA analogue: designees act within written limits of authority and the FAA retains oversight (2.3).
- **Why it works.** Limits make "may I decide this?" answerable without judgment calls, scale authority with risk, and make exceptions visible. Segregation of duties prevents one party's error or fraud passing unchecked.
- **Known gaming (memory).** Splitting a purchase into pieces below the approval limit. Aggregate limits and "related transactions" rules exist to catch this.
- **Translation: the autonomy matrix.**
  - Rows = decision type (approve ECR as-is, approve with conditions, reject, request info, re-sequence in roll-up). Columns = conditions: unit-cost delta, annualised cost, effectivity slip in days, forecast confidence, new/unqualified supplier, safety or certification-relevant part, shared-resource conflict with another open ECR, age of newest source.
  - Each cell: `auto-approve` / `auto-approve + sampled second read` / `recommend only` / `escalate to <role>`. Defaults conservative.
  - Cumulative caps: per agent seat per week/quarter, and per part or supplier across related ECRs, so splitting a change into five small ECRs cannot slip under the limit.
  - The matrix is versioned data; every decision stores the row id and matrix version that authorised it. Changing it is a human-only action by a role distinct from the people who benefit from faster approvals, logged.
  - Segregation of duties: the agent that estimated an assumption cannot be the sole approver of a decision that rests on it; the agent cannot edit its own limits, matrix, or review of its work.
  - Authority is earned: start at recommend-only, widen a cell when a track record (forecast vs actual, human override rate) supports it. Record the reasons.

## 3. Synthesis: 10 design rules

1. **The stranger test is the acceptance criterion.** Someone competent but not present must be able to reconstruct sources, assumptions, calculations, reasoning and authority from the UI alone, in minutes. (AS 1215.06A; law's case file; science reproducibility.)
2. **Every claim has an evidence class and a pointer.** Four classes, visibly different: Fact (source snapshot id, version, retrieved-at), Assumption (origin, rationale, impact-if-wrong), Computation (code version, input hash, seed, re-run button), Judgment (reasoning, alternatives, indicators). Tickmarks on numbers show the class. (Audit tickmarks and cross-reference; ICD 203 Std 1 and 3; SOAP; journalism; science.)
3. **Separate likelihood from confidence, use fixed words, and show the source summary.** Range = likelihood; confidence = evidence quality; one-line "rests mainly on X and Y" per decision. Avoid false precision on agent-estimated inputs. (ICD 203 Std 1, 2; ICD 206.)
4. **Plan first, record negatives and alternatives.** Work plan before compute, plan-versus-actual diff, searches that found nothing, options rejected and why; post hoc analyses labelled exploratory. (Pre-registration; ICD 203 Std 4; SOAP pertinent negatives; AS 1215.12-.13.)
5. **The platform writes the record, and sign-offs bind a version.** Append-only, tamper-evident (hash chained), written by the harness, never by the agent. Approvals attach to the artifact hash; later changes are additions with who, when and why; edits void approvals. Snapshots of inputs, not links. (Flight recorders; 14 CFR 43.9; AS 1215.16; lab notebooks; chain of custody.)
6. **Maker and checker are different, and the checker forms its view first.** Blind independent second read on anything auto-approved above a sampling threshold, human arbiter on disagreement; the agent never grades its own homework. (Radiology double reading; Form 8130-9 conformity; segregation of duties; AS 1220.)
7. **Autonomy is a versioned matrix with cumulative caps, not a toggle.** Recommend vs approve, per-condition limits, aggregate caps per supplier/part/period, row and matrix version logged on every decision, widened by track record. (DoA matrices; COSO; FAA DER limits; 2.8.)
8. **Escalate by named trigger, in a structured message.** Triggers: over limit, linchpin assumption is agent-estimated with low confidence, sources conflict, novel part/supplier, conflicting open ECR, stale data, failed cite-check, own check disagreed. Message is SBAR-shaped and ends with a specific ask. Any agent or checker can raise the flag and nobody downstream can silently clear it. (SBAR; CRM challenge; I-PASS; ICD 203.)
9. **Spawned researchers are staff, and the supervisor's review is on the record.** Each sub-agent gets a written brief (question, scope, constraints, read-back), returns structured findings with its own cited sources, and the parent logs what it accepted, rejected or re-checked, as an auditor reviews a specialist's work. The tree is drillable: decision, sub-agent, sources it read. (I-PASS; legal partner/associate review; AS 1215 reviewer sign-off and use of specialists.)
10. **Design the trail to be read: exceptions first, then layered drill-down, then stranger-proof detail.** The card opens with the bottom line and a "check these three things first" list ranked by sensitivity times assumption weakness, then per-number drill-down, then raw log. Mechanical cite-check blocks fabricated sources. (Legal memo format; audit review notes; alarm-fatigue lessons; Mata v. Avianca.)

## 4. Pitfalls these fields learned the hard way

- **Logs nobody reads, alerts everyone overrides.** Medication safety alerts were overridden in 49-96% of cases in the van der Sijs review of 1980-2004 literature (snippet, secondary); the Joint Commission (Sentinel Event Alert 50, 2013; snippet) estimates 85-99% of device alarms need no clinical action. If we flag everything, nothing is flagged. Rank by consequence, cap the number of flags on one card, and measure how often humans actually open drill-downs.
- **Checklists become ritual.** Haynes et al. (NEJM 2009) saw mortality fall from 1.5% to 0.8% with the WHO surgical checklist, while Urbach et al. (NEJM 2014) found no significant change across 101 Ontario hospitals after mandated use (snippet; debated, short follow-up). Documentation that is completed but not engaged with gives no safety. Do not count "reviewed" without evidence of drill-down or a substantive comment.
- **Automation complacency and bias.** Parasuraman and Manzey (Human Factors 2010) show that under multiple-task load, operators attend less to the automated task and delay detecting its failures (snippet); Bainbridge's "Ironies of Automation" (1983; memory) argues that the more reliable the automation, the less practised and attentive the monitor. Counter with planted errors (the Hyphenate brief asks for an answer key anyway), random sampled deep-reviews, and showing evidence before the agent's conclusion in the review flow.
- **Delegation under pressure.** The 2020 House Transportation Committee report on the 737 MAX documented conflicts of interest in the system that deputises manufacturer employees, authorised representatives failing to pass information to the FAA, and perceived "undue pressure" from management; a separate FAA-commissioned panel kept delegation but called for freedom from undue pressure; findings were contested (search results). Lesson: the party that benefits from faster approvals must not control the limits, the checker or the escalation path. Log every change to thresholds and who made it.
- **Splitting to stay under the limit.** Standard procurement abuse (memory). Aggregate caps, related-item grouping, and a flag on bursts of small ECRs against one part.
- **Back-filled or rationalised documentation.** Audit rules fix a completion date and require dated additions (AS 1215.15-.16) because files get tidied after the fact. For us the special risk is that an LLM's written explanation may not be the real cause of its decision (Turpin et al. 2023 and Anthropic's 2025 work on reasoning-model faithfulness; memory). Treat the model's prose as a claim to be checked: the proof is the recorded inputs, code path and cited rows, not the narrative. Never show an after-the-fact "why" without the primary trace next to it.
- **Fabricated or stale citations.** Mata v. Avianca. Verify citations mechanically and mark stale ones when the base moves ("March changes June").
- **Second readers who are not independent.** Radiology reviews found second readers often see the first reader's result (snippet). Keep the checker blind until it has recorded its own view, and use a different prompt or model.
- **False precision and vocabulary drift.** A 2024 ODNI-released research paper argues the IC has not used confidence levels as intended and that they can convey false precision (snippet). Do not print p10/p90 to three digits when the distribution is an agent guess; show the origin next to it.
- **Links rot, systems change, people leave.** "No matter how long ago" means snapshots, retained inputs and version hashes (AS 1215 seven-year retention). A link to a live ERP row is not evidence.
- **Unreadable trail volume.** Hundreds of pages of log are as unusable as none. Keep three tiers (summary, per-claim drill-down, raw log) and make sure the first two are enough for a lunch-break read.
