# Product naming

Researched 2026-10-24 by an Opus agent. Conflict checks are one or two web searches per name,
plus a DNS lookup on the domain. A domain that resolves is registered; one that doesn't resolve
may still be registered. "(memory)" marks a claim I didn't search for.

**Decision. Tyler, 2026-10-24: Norn, with Orrery second.**

**Norn: no serious conflict found.** The nearest is a small product that shares the name and the
broad space: Norn Data, Inc.'s Norn, a live beta AI investment-research app for individual
investors. Every short Norn domain is taken. Details are in the Norn conflict check below.

Caveats:
- In Norse myth the Norns decide fate rather than estimate it. That fits "sees what's coming"
  better than "informed estimation".
- Said aloud in a noisy hall, "Norn" can be misheard as "Norm". It helps to say "Norn, as in the
  Norse fates" the first time.
- If it became a company, it would need a qualifier on the domain or the name.

## Orrery (second choice)

- **Origin:** An orrery is a clockwork model of the solar system that shows where the planets
  will be.
- **Conflicts found in round 2:**
  - Sandvar's [Orrery](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/sandvar) is a platform for
    building AI agent teams that run on local machines.
  - [Orrerys](https://startup-seeker.com/company/orrerys~com) (London, 2025) is an AI portfolio
    tracker for new investors, at orrerys.com.
  - A 2018 [Orrery.ai](https://www.geekwire.com/tag/orrery-ai/) venture aimed at data for finance;
    I found no later coverage of it.
- **Domains:** orrery.com and orrery.ai resolve to what look like domain-parking hosts, so they
  are probably parked and may be for sale. I didn't confirm that.
- **Verdict:** Its conflicts are closer to us than Norn's (an AI agent platform, and an AI finance
  app), which supports Norn as first choice.

## Round 1: plain descriptive names

Tyler's brief: pitched to finance, the goal is visibility, and engineers get the hard part done for
them, "kind of like giving your boss a report". He'd already rejected Ripple, Downstream and Cut-in
as too engineering-flavoured.

The top 5 were PreRead, Lookahead, Costsight, Changebrief and Prospectus. Tyler rejected them as
"really basic".

Cut for conflicts:
- **Changecast:** [changecast.ai](https://changecast.ai) is a live AI product writing role
  briefings, finance included.
- **Glidepath:** [glidepath.ai](https://glidepath.ai) is an exchange fund.
- **Sightline:** Manhattan Associates
  [launched Sightline](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20260728ae14586/) in July 2026,
  for explainable supply-chain forecasts.
- **Preflight:** xtype sells a
  [Preflight AI agent](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250430450553/en).
- **Farsight:** [Farsight](https://www.axios.com/pro/fintech-deals/2025/06/11/farsight-16-million-investment-workflows)
  sells AI agents for finance.

## Round 2: abstract names in the Oracle mould

Tyler's brief: "words that don't have as much meaning attached, or are more abstract.
Something Oracle-esque: Oracle is this big watcher looking over everything that also maybe knows
about the future." It shouldn't read as a Palantir knock-off, since Archer uses Palantir Foundry.

Mid-round he added a second angle, prediction through educated research: "we don't really know
the future, but we're trying to know the future."
